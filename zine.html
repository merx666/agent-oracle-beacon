5. AI at the Landfill & Scrapyard: Real-World Material Triage, E-Waste Gold Recovery, and When to Strip vs. Sell Bulk

Listen to tech podcasts and you’d think AI is only used by software engineers in glass office towers writing emails to each other. Meanwhile, out in the real physical economy, I work at a landfill—and my boss uses AI every single day to evaluate the material streams rolling across the tipping floor and figure out what they are actually worth.

When industrial machinery, commercial HVAC units, buckets of insulated wire, or pallets of old computers come into a landfill or salvage yard, you aren't just looking at "trash." You are looking at a real-time commodity optimization puzzle where labor time, shop space, hazardous processing, and scrap metal spot prices collide:

"Is there a buyer for this exact component as-is? Should we toss it straight onto the scrapyard truck in raw form, pay someone to strip it down to bare copper and aluminum, or box up the circuit boards and sell them in bulk to a specialist who scrapes gold from old computers?"

⚖️ The 3-Way Scrapyard & Landfill Triage Matrix (Powered by AI)

  • Path 1: Sell Raw to the Metal Scrapyard (Zero Stripping Labor) — Sometimes the smartest move is not touching a wrench at all. If you get a load of sealed compressors, rusted mixed appliances, or low-grade irony aluminum where stripping takes 40 minutes of labor to gain $6 in spread, AI quickly runs the weight-to-grade math and tells you: haul it straight to the scrapyard scale as dirty/mixed bulk and take the instant check.
  • Path 2: Strip Down to Basic Commodities In-House (High-Margin Deconstruction) — When heavy copper transformers, brass commercial plumbing, thick jacketed feeder cable, or clean extruded aluminum frames come in, the spread between "dirty breakage" and clean separated grades (#1 Bright Bare Copper, #2 Copper, clean Yellow Brass, 6061 Aluminum) is massive. AI helps identify alloy grades, estimate internal copper winding weights before you even cut the housing open, and confirm that 20 minutes with a sawzall or wire stripper multiplies the payout 3x to 5x.
  • Path 3: Sell Bulk Sub-Assemblies to Specialist Refiners (The E-Waste Gold Strategy) — What about pallets of old desktop towers, server blades, telecom boards, gold-finger RAM sticks, and vintage ceramic CPUs? There is real gold, palladium, and silver on those boards—but building your own chemical deconstruction and acid-refining workshop is a massive trap if you're running a landfill operation. Instead, AI identifies the exact board tiers from photos and part numbers (high-grade telecom vs. mid-grade motherboard vs. low-grade power supply board), calculates the precious-metal yield value per pound, and helps you package and sell the raw boards in bulk directly to dedicated gold-recovery specialists who already have the refining equipment.

That’s what real-world AI looks like outside the Silicon Valley bubble: turning a chaotic pile of discarded steel, copper, and silicon into instant clarity on whether to scrap it raw, strip it clean, or wholesale it to a specialist without wasting a single hour of shop labor.

6. When They Paywalled the Guitar Pro 5 Commons: How Musicians Built the Tab Archives for Free—and Used AI to Take Them Back

If you played guitar in the 2000s when Guitar Pro 5 (.gp3, .gp4, .gp5) was king, you remember the golden age of the internet music commons. Thousands of everyday guitarists—myself included—spent countless late nights hunched over an amp and a keyboard, transcribing entire albums note-for-note, solo-for-solo, drum-track-by-drum-track, and uploading those Guitar Pro files to free community archives so anyone in the world could download them and learn.

Nobody asked for a dime. We wrote and shared those tabs because we loved music and wanted the next kid picking up a guitar to have the blueprints.

"Then some slick aggregator came along, scraped the entire free community archive that musicians like us had transcribed by hand, built a web player to render the tabs in the browser, and slapped a paywall on the download button—charging us to access the very files we gave the world for free."

Here is the irony of how modern web enclosures work: to display those interactive tablature staves and play the backing tracks natively inside your web browser without Guitar Pro installed, the website’s own front-end player still has to transmit the underlying tab file into your browser's memory so the client-side JavaScript renderer can draw the notes on your screen. They didn't invent the music or transcribe the notes—they just hid the raw community file behind an obfuscated network call and locked the "Download" button behind a credit card prompt.

Before AI, figuring out how a complex single-page web player obscures its asset requests meant digging through thousands of lines of minified JavaScript by hand. Instead, I sat down with my AI agent, pointed it at the page structure and network flow, and used AI to trace exactly where the browser player was stashing the underlying Guitar Pro file data to render the screen. Within minutes, the AI wrote a clean browser script that injected a direct download button right onto the screen—restoring one-click access to the free community transcriptions that belonged to the public commons in the first place.

Even better, once we pushed that button and downloaded the raw Guitar Pro 5 (.gp5) file, we didn't even need proprietary software to unlock its full value. We pulled the downloaded .gp5 file straight into a free open-source Guitar Pro-compatible app (like TuxGuitar), isolated the individual instrument tracks, and extracted the raw MIDI data. Having the unlocked tablature and multi-track MIDI allowed me to break down the exact voicings, learn the music inside and out, and play those songs live in a cover band—turning a paywalled web page back into real, living music on a stage.
